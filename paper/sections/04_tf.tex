\subsection{From configuration to operating parameters}
With $a=R_0/A$ and inboard build $w_b$, the inner-leg outer radius and the
outer-leg inner radius are
\begin{equation}
  R_1 = R_0-a-w_b,\qquad R_2=(R_0+a)\,\delta^{-1/N_\mathrm{TF}},
\end{equation}
where $\delta$ is the allowed ripple at the plasma edge
\todo{state the ripple approximation and its validity}. The current per
coil is $NI = 2\pi R_0 B_0/(\mu_0 N_\mathrm{TF})$. In MADE~1.0 the peak
field was obtained from Amp\`ere's law at $R_1$ with a ripple correction
and a linear gradient across the winding pack \cite{Giannini2023MADE}.
The Amp\`ere field is evaluated at the plasma side of the winding pack,
$B_\mathrm{Amp}=\mu_0 N_\mathrm{TF} NI/[2\pi(R_1-\Delta r_\mathrm{ps})]$,
while the field at $R_1$, multiplied by the ripple correction, returns
the field on axis as a consistency check.

\paragraph{Calibrated discrete field model (new in 2.0).}
The smeared linear profile under-predicts the inner layers by up to
2--3~T. At the start of each scan MADE~2.0 builds reference winding packs
for every toroidal WP width $W$ and a set of currents, computes the field
of the discrete turns with a 2D model validated against ANSYS, and stores
a peak factor $k(W,I_\mathrm{op})$ and a per-layer profile
$f(s;W,I_\mathrm{op})$, $s$ being the fraction of turns enclosed. Grade
$k$ is then sized at $B_k=k\,B_\mathrm{Amp}\,f(s_k)$; a discrete
verification of each candidate is optional. Because $k$ is defined as a
ratio to $B_\mathrm{Amp}$, the sizing field does not depend on the
Amp\`ere approximation.
\todo{Fig.: smeared vs calibrated vs FE field per layer.}

The coil is assumed to follow a bending-free (Princeton-D) shape between
$R_1$ and $R_2$, with $k=\tfrac12\ln(R_2/R_1)$
\cite{File1971,Gralnick1976,Thome1982}. This gives in
closed form the centring-free hoop tension
$T=\tfrac12\,k\,N_\mathrm{TF}\,\mu_0 (NI)^2/(2\pi)$ and the inductance
\begin{equation}
L=\frac{\mu_0 (N_t k)^2 \sqrt{R_1R_2}}{2N_\mathrm{TF}}
\big[I_0(k)+2I_1(k)+I_2(k)\big].
\end{equation}
In MADE~2.0 the analytical D is the starting point of an iteration
\cite{Giannini2024MADEIII}: the field of the discrete set of $N_\mathrm{TF}$
coils is computed by Biot--Savart on the centreline, and the shape is
updated by solving $\rho = N/(\tfrac12 I B)$ until the tension $N$ is
constant along the coil. The resulting shape feeds the PF placement
(section~\ref{sec:cspf}) and the global model
(section~\ref{sec:stellarator}).

\subsection{Case and inner vault}
The nose thickness is increased until both the vault and the innermost
jacket satisfy their Tresca allowables,
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_\mathrm{T,vault}=\sigma_z+\frac{2}{1-\beta^2}\,p_\mathrm{m}\,
  d_\mathrm{vault} \le \sigma_\mathrm{allow},\qquad
  \sigma_\mathrm{T,jacket}=\sigma_z+\sigma_\mathrm{rm}\,d_\mathrm{WP}
  \le \sigma_\mathrm{allow},
\end{equation}
with $\beta=R_k/R_1$, $\sigma_z=T/(A_\mathrm{jacket}+A_\mathrm{case})$ and
$d_\mathrm{vault}$, $d_\mathrm{WP}$ load-sharing factors from the
toroidal and radial stiffness of vault, case and winding pack.
In MADE~2.0 these analytical load-sharing factors are complemented by
FE-calibrated surrogates of the jacket and case stresses
\todo{describe wp\_mech\_surrogate and its calibration range}.
\textbf{The wedged inner vault is what makes the tokamak TF tractable with
a 1D/2D model}: the centring force is reacted by the vault in toroidal
compression. This is precisely the assumption that fails for stellarator
coils.
