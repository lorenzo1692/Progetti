\section{Towards stellarators: a global beam--shell model}
\label{sec:stellarator}
% This section is the forward-looking part of the paper: it states the
% method and shows first results of the global model on a tokamak TF
% D-coil (where a reference exists), and the plan for stellarators.

\subsection{Why the TF approach does not transfer directly}
Stellarator coils are non-planar and carry, besides the in-plane forces of
a TF coil, large out-of-plane and locally varying net forces. There is no
wedged inner vault: each coil is held by a coil case and by an
inter-coil support structure, and the loads cannot be reduced to a
membrane hoop tension plus a centring force. The 1D/2D radial-build
treatment of section~\ref{sec:tf} is therefore not applicable as is.

\subsection{Proposed approach}
We split the problem in two levels:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Local level (conductor and winding pack).} The cable and the
winding pack are designed exactly as in the TF solver --- same conductor
module, same jacket sizing, same grading on the local peak field --- but
\emph{without the wedge}: the winding pack cross-section is no longer
bounded by the wedge angle $2\pi/N_\mathrm{TF}$, but by a prescribed
rectangular envelope around the filament (the coil-to-coil and
coil-to-plasma clearances from the coil optimisation).
\item \textbf{Global level (structure).} The coil and its support are
modelled with beams along the coil centreline (winding pack + case,
smeared section properties from the local level) and shells for the
inter-coil structure. Line loads come from Biot--Savart on the full coil
set. The model returns section forces and moments along the coil, which
are fed back to the local level to check jacket and case stresses.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Beam--shell formulation}
The global model (\texttt{coil3d/tf3d\_global\_model.m},
\texttt{tf3d\_global\_solve.m}) represents every coil of the system as a
closed chain of 3D Timoshenko beams along its centreline, with a hollow
rectangular section standing for the case, and the inter-coil structures
as four-node shells (membrane with incompatible modes, Mindlin plate,
drilling stiffness). For a tokamak TF system the shells model the wedged
vault on the straight inner leg and the outer inter-coil structures (OIS);
each coil has a gravity support. Loads are nodal forces from Biot--Savart
on the coil set, rotated to every coil or given coil by coil. The beam
orientation follows the coil (toroidal direction for a planar TF), which
for a non-planar coil becomes the finite-build frame of the coil
filament instead of the Frenet frame, ill-defined where the curvature
vanishes.

\paragraph{Section from the selected MADE design.} The beam section is not
an input: it is the inner-leg cross-section of the design point selected
in the TF solver, integrated numerically. The case is the wedge sector
$|x|\le y\tan(\pi/N_\mathrm{TF})$ between the bore arc and the
plasma-side face, minus the winding-pack cavity (layers widened by the
ground insulation, as in the 2D FE model); the jackets of each layer are
smeared over the layer and weighted by $E_J/E_\mathrm{case}$. Cable,
insulation and filler carry no load, consistently with the steel area of
the 2D sizing ($\sigma_z=T/(A_\mathrm{jacket}+A_\mathrm{case})$). The
section gives the area, the in-plane and out-of-plane bending stiffnesses
about the centroid, the torsional constant (Bredt's formula on the closed
case box) and the extreme-fibre distances. The vault shells take the
radial steel crossing the coil mid-plane (nose, plasma-side plate and
jacket walls), so that the 2D and 3D models share one geometry.

\paragraph{Coupling with the 2D sizing.} The 2D sizing loads the inner
leg with the bending-free tension $T$; the global model gives the force
the inner leg actually carries (beam axial force plus vault membrane
force over one chord). Their ratio, the axial load factor
$k=F_\mathrm{inner}/T$, is fed back to every sizing step and the loop
sizing $\leftrightarrow$ global model is iterated to convergence
(about 1.2 on the reference design).

\paragraph{Outer intercoil structures chosen and checked before the run.}
The OIS are not found by a radius window as in the reference ANSYS model,
but declared by the designer as panels between adjacent coils, each with
a position along the centreline (fractions $s_1$--$s_2$ of the coil
length) and a thickness. Before the solution the code checks that the
panels do not overlap each other or the wedged vault, that the cases are
actually apart there (free width $2R\sin(\pi/N_\mathrm{TF})-W>0$, else the
region is wedged), that the panel is not thicker than that free width, and
that it keeps a clearance from the PF/CS coils; it then plots the
centreline with the panels and asks for confirmation.

\paragraph{Element verification.} Cantilever beam under axial, two
bending and torsion loads: exact; membrane patch test on a distorted
mesh: exact; pure in-plane bending of one element row: exact; plate
cantilever with 8 elements: $-0.4$\% w.r.t. Euler--Bernoulli.

\subsection{Adapting the beam--shell model to stellarator coils}
The model was written for an arbitrary centreline (nodes in 3D, beams
with an orientation vector, shells between coils), so the extension
concerns the inputs rather than the solver.

\paragraph{Centreline and section frame.} Each coil is read as a filament
(the output of the coil optimisation) and resampled. The beam orientation
vector is the toroidal-like normal of the finite-build frame of the coil
(the frame in which the optimisation defines the winding-pack
rectangle), constant on each element; the Frenet frame is avoided because
it is undefined where the curvature vanishes and flips at inflection
points.

\paragraph{Section without wedge.} The local level is the TF chain without
the wedge (section~\ref{sec:tf}): the winding pack is a rectangle around
the filament, sized on the local peak field, and the case a box of
prescribed wall thicknesses around it. The section routine is the same
as for the TF coil with the trapezoid replaced by a rectangle, and the
same modulus-weighted, Bredt and shear-area definitions. Since the
cross-section does not change along a stellarator coil, one section per
coil type is used; if the case is locally reinforced, the section can be
assigned per element.

\paragraph{Loads and symmetry.} Line loads come from Biot--Savart on the
complete coil set (finite build, multi-filament near the coil), with PF
coils and plasma current when relevant. Unlike the tokamak TF system, the
loads differ from coil to coil: they are computed for the unique coils of
one half period and mapped to the others by the field-period rotation and
by stellarator symmetry (rotation by $\pi$ about a horizontal axis), and
applied coil by coil.

\paragraph{Intercoil structure.} There is no wedged vault: all the
centring and out-of-plane loads go through the case and the intercoil
structure, which becomes the main structural element. It is modelled with
the same shells, generalising the OIS panels of the TF case: (i) panels
are declared per pair of adjacent coils, since the coils differ; (ii) the
nodes of two different coils do not correspond, so each panel connects
nodes matched by closest-point projection along the two centrelines, and
warped quadrilaterals are split in two triangles; (iii) the pre-run checks
use the actual distance between the case surfaces of the two coils
instead of $2R\sin(\pi/N_\mathrm{TF})-W$, and keep-out zones for ports,
diagnostics and heating lines replace the PF clearance. Gravity supports
and, if present, a central support ring close the model.

\paragraph{Back to the conductor.} Section forces $(N,V_y,V_z,T,M_y,M_z)$
along each coil are converted into stresses in the winding pack and in
the case with the same modulus-weighted section, $\sigma_{L,k}=E_{L,k}/E_\mathrm{ref}\,
(N/A + M_z y/I_z + M_y x/I_y)$, plus the local magnetic pressure on the
turns; jacket and case are checked against the same Tresca allowables as
the TF solver, and the case walls or the intercoil panels are thickened
where they fail.

\paragraph{Status and verification path.} The solver, the section from
the design and the OIS controls are implemented and verified on the
tokamak TF system (table~\ref{tab:global}); the stellarator inputs
(filament reader, finite-build frame, coil-by-coil symmetry, panel
matching between different coils) are the next step.
